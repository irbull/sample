On a rainy evening in a tiny harbor town, Mira found a brass key tucked inside an old library book.

The key had no label, only a star etched on one side. Curious, she wandered the cobblestone streets until she noticed the same star on a weathered blue door behind the clocktower.

Inside was a narrow room filled with maps, journals, and a lantern that lit itself when she stepped in. On the desk sat a note:

"If you found the key, the next chapter is yours to write."

Mira smiled, opened a blank journal, and began.
She wrote exactly three words before the lantern coughed out a puff of glitter and projected a floating checklist in midair:

1. Find treasure
2. Defeat curse
3. Submit expense report

"Expense report?" Mira said.

A drawer popped open and launched a rubber stamp into her hand. It read: APPROVED BY MYSTERIOUS FORCES. Another drawer slid out with thirty-seven receipts, including one for "Emergency Croissant — strategic." 

As she tried to understand line item "Two (2) grappling hooks, artisanal," the blue door creaked and a very round orange cat waddled in wearing a tiny captain's hat.

"Ah good," said the cat. "You’re the new Archivist. We’ve been short-staffed since Gerald got promoted to Legend."

Mira blinked. "You can talk?"

"Only during business hours," said the cat, glancing at a pocket watch. "After six I mostly yowl about tuna and the futility of monarchy."

He hopped onto the desk, pushed the receipts aside, and pointed at a map with his tail.

"Tonight’s mission," he announced, "is to recover the Lost Compass of Slightly Better Directions."

"What does it do?" Mira asked.

"Instead of saying 'You are here,' it says, 'You are probably here, unless vibes are off.'"

Mira nodded solemnly, because this seemed like exactly the kind of quest one accepts when handed enchanted office supplies by a speaking maritime cat.

She stamped the first page of her journal, packed a croissant for tactical reasons, and followed Captain Marmalade into the moonlit alley—

—where they were immediately confronted by three seagulls in neckties demanding to see their permits.
